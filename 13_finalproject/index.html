<div class="textcontainer"> <p class="margin"></p> <h3>Final Project: Smart Water Harvester w/ Modular Material Tiles</h3> <h4> My final project is a <b>Smart Fog Harvester</b> designed to explore how <b>material properties, surface texture, and geometry</b> affect water collection. Instead of focusing purely on automation, the project centers the <b>tactile and physical behavior of materials</b>—how kerfing, wettability, and surface design shape efficiency. </h4> <p> The harvester consists of a <b>laser-cut frame</b> that holds multiple interchangeable <b>tiles</b>. Each tile tests a different surface treatment or material behavior. An Arduino-based sensing system measures the water mass collected over time, as well as ambient humidity and temperature. If time allows, I’ll implement a <b>servo-controlled tilting mechanism</b> to optimize the collection angle automatically. </p> <h4>Tile Designs (Materials Experiments)</h4> <ol> <li><b>Kerfed Wood Fin Tile</b> — Thin birch sheet laser-cut with varying kerf densities so the panel naturally curves into spines. Hydrophilic/hydrophobic stripes will be created using PVA glue (hydrophilic) and silicone spray (hydrophobic). This explores how curvature and wettability patterns guide droplet flow.</li> <li><b>Ribbed PLA Panel</b> — A 3D-printed PLA sheet with diagonal V-grooves and ridges that channel droplets downward. Some regions will be lightly sanded (more hydrophilic), others left smooth (more hydrophobic), testing how groove direction and surface roughness affect yield.</li> <li><b>Flexible Mesh Tile</b> — A 3D-printed TPU mesh or nylon fabric framed in acrylic. I’ll coat mesh nodes with PVA to encourage droplet nucleation and leave strands untreated or silicone-coated for quick release. This examines the balance between droplet growth and shedding.</li> <li><b>Beetle-Back Dot Tile</b> — A flat PLA sheet with hemispherical bumps inspired by the Namib desert beetle. Hydrophilic bump tops and hydrophobic valleys will demonstrate how contrasting wettability drives droplet movement.</li> </ol> <p> Each tile mounts at a ~45° angle on a common frame above a 3D-printed V-gutter that funnels collected water into a small reservoir resting on a <b>load cell</b>. The setup allows A/B testing by swapping tiles under identical fog and airflow conditions. </p> <h4>Electronics and Control System</h4> <ul> <li><b>Arduino Nano</b> — central microcontroller</li> <li><b>HX711 + Load Cell</b> — measures harvested water mass (grams)</li> <li><b>DHT22 Sensor</b> — records humidity and temperature for each trial</li> <li><b>Servo Motor (SG90)</b> — adjusts panel tilt angle (for optimization mode)</li> <li><b>SSD1306 OLED Display</b> — shows live yield rate, humidity, and temperature</li> <li><b>Fan (PWM-controlled)</b> — provides consistent airflow across runs</li> <li><b>Optional MicroSD Card Module</b> — logs mass and environmental data over time</li> </ul> <p> In basic mode, the system logs water yield (g/min) and environmental conditions over time. In advanced mode, the Arduino automatically adjusts the tilt angle to maximize collection efficiency, then displays and stores results. </p> <h4>Bill of Materials</h4> <table border="1" cellpadding="4"> <tr><th>Component</th><th>Quantity</th><th>Source / Material</th></tr> <tr><td>Arduino Nano</td><td>1</td><td>Electronics stock</td></tr> <tr><td>HX711 Load Cell Amplifier</td><td>1</td><td>Electronics stock</td></tr> <tr><td>Load Cell (1–5 kg)</td><td>1</td><td>Amazon / PS70 stock</td></tr> <tr><td>DHT22 Sensor</td><td>1</td><td>PS70 stock</td></tr> <tr><td>SG90 Servo Motor</td><td>1</td><td>PS70 stock</td></tr> <tr><td>SSD1306 OLED Display</td><td>1</td><td>PS70 stock</td></tr> <tr><td>5V Fan (PWM)</td><td>1</td><td>PS70 stock</td></tr> <tr><td>MicroSD Card Module + Card</td><td>1 (optional)</td><td>PS70 stock</td></tr> <tr><td>Laser-cut Birch Plywood (1/8")</td><td>~1 sheet</td><td>Shop stock</td></tr> <tr><td>PLA Filament</td><td>~100 g</td><td>Shop stock</td></tr> <tr><td>TPU Filament or Nylon Mesh</td><td>~50 g / 6×6” piece</td><td>Shop stock</td></tr> <tr><td>PVA Glue, Silicone Spray</td><td>—</td><td>Shop / hardware store</td></tr> <tr><td>Wires, Breadboard, Jumper Cables</td><td>—</td><td>Lab stock</td></tr> </table> <h4>3D Model Overview</h4> <p> The 3D model shows the fog harvester’s frame, mounting bay for interchangeable tiles, servo-controlled hinge, and a lower gutter channel leading into the load cell platform.
